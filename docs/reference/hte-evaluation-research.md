# HTE evaluation research (CMO 104, student feedback, fair aggregation)

Researched 2026-10-24 for IDSMS. Not legal advice: check the privacy and libel points with SLU's Data Protection Officer.

**Source caveat:** ched.gov.ph returns HTTP 403 to automated fetches. The CMO 104 quotes below come from a verbatim copy in the [CarSU internship manual](https://sustainability.carsu.edu.ph/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Manual-on-Occupational-Internship-Program_compressed.pdf), whose OCR scrambled the list letters in Sec. 14.2.2, so quotes are cited by section number only. Check them against the signed CHED PDF before quoting in the thesis.

## Summary

- CMO 104 requires the HEI to evaluate the **HTE itself**, with a feedback mechanism (Sec. 14.2.2).
- It does **not** name a *student* evaluation of the HTE. Student feedback is a reasonable way to meet the duty, but it is not mandated.
- No form or criteria for evaluating the HTE are prescribed. The annexes cover the internship plan, MOA, annual report, HTE and intern list, and contract.
- Common criteria: supervision, relevance and learning, safety and welfare, communication, integration and treatment, resources, and "would you recommend".
- Aggregates: n=3 is the "absolute minimum"; 5–10 is common. Small cohorts also need complementary suppression.
- Named supervisors are data subjects under RA 10173, so lawful basis, proportionality and the right to dispute inaccuracies apply.
- Public, malicious imputations against a company are libel, and online they become cyber-libel with a heavier penalty. Keep feedback fact-based and internal.

## 1. CMO No. 104, s. 2017: HEI duties toward HTEs

- **Sec. 10 / 14.1.2 / 14.2.1:** agreements "only with duly licensed/accredited HTEs"; "a pool of reputable HTEs"; "Select the HTEs and ensure acceptability of internship plan and internship venues in order to protect student intern interest".
- **Sec. 14.2.2 (Monitoring and Evaluation):** "Conduct initial and regular visit/inspection of their HTE to ensure safety of student interns"; "**Conduct monitoring and evaluation of the HTE to gauge the overall performance of HTE and provide feedback mechanism**"; "Conduct a post training review and evaluation of the program and the performance as well as with the partner HTE".
- **Sec. 14.2.3:** annual CHEDRO report on interns' "issues and concerns and actions to be taken" (Annex C), plus MOA copies and a certified HTE and intern list (Annex D).
- **Sec. 15.2.3:** the coordinator must "Inspect internship venues and sites".
- **Sec. 16.2.9 / 16.2.11:** the intern's journal records "any problem/s encountered", and the intern reports "for an exit assessment". This is the closest the CMO comes to student feedback.
- **Sec. 20.2:** "the HTE evaluation report will be the basis of the student intern's final grade". This is the HTE's evaluation *of the intern*.
- **Sec. 24.8–24.9 (violations):** "Failing to monitor periodically … welfare of student interns", and failing to report "results of pre and post monitoring and evaluation".

**Answer:** CMO 104 says nothing about who evaluates the HTE or how. A student evaluation is permitted, not required. UP CIDS calls CMO 104 "primarily … a checklist for legitimacy" ([Santillana 2026](https://cids.up.edu.ph/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Proposed-Quality-Internship-Framework-for-Host-Training-Establishments-HTEs.pdf)).

## 3. Fair aggregation into a company profile

**Thresholds.** US Dept. of Education PTAC: "Many statisticians consider a cell size of 3 to be the absolute minimum … larger minimums (e.g., 5 or 10) may be used", "with a majority of States using 10". Also, "complementary suppression of additional non-sensitive cells may be necessary" ([PTAC FAQ](https://studentprivacy.ed.gov/sites/default/files/resource_document/file/FAQs_disclosure_avoidance_0.pdf)). For IDSMS: hide below 5 or more, pool across terms, and avoid splits that isolate one student.

**Facts vs opinions.** Store hours, task types and incident flags as structured fields, separate from free text. The Supreme Court protects opinion "based on established facts" but not "a false allegation of fact" ([Borjal v. CA, 1999](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1999/jan1999/gr_126466_1999.html)). *Caveat:* Borjal involved a public official, so whether it extends to reviews of private companies is not verified.

**Defamation.** Libel is "a public and malicious imputation … tending to cause the dishonor, discredit, or contempt of a natural or juridical person". It is presumed malicious "even if it be true", except "A private communication … in the performance of any legal, moral or social duty" ([RPC Arts. 353–354](https://lawphil.net/statutes/acts/act1930/act_3815_1930.html)). Online, the penalty is "one (1) degree higher" ([RA 10175 Secs. 4(c)(4), 6](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2012/ra_10175_2012.html)). *My inference, not verified:* coordinator-only access under the CMO 104 duty fits that private-duty exception better than showing feedback to all students.

**Right of reply.** CMO 104 already requires a feedback mechanism toward the HTE (Sec. 14.2.2). UP CIDS found HTEs "are rarely provided with feedback regarding their strengths and weaknesses". A company response field follows from both, though no primary source requires one.

**RA 10173** ([text](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2012/ra_10173_2012.html)):
- A named supervisor is personal information, identity "reasonably and directly ascertained" (Sec. 3(g)). The company itself is a juridical person and outside the Act.
- Lawful basis: legitimate interest (the CMO 104 duty), "not overridden" by the data subject's rights (Sec. 12(f)).
- Data must be "adequate and not excessive" (Sec. 11(d)). Rate the role, not the person, and redact names in free text.
- People may "be informed" (Sec. 16(a)), get access (16(c)) and "dispute the inaccuracy or error" (16(d)). Cover HTE feedback in the privacy notice and give named people a dispute path.
- Do not rely on the Sec. 4(d) research exemption for an operational system. *Not verified:* the IRR's separate right to object. The statute has only Sec. 16(e) blocking/removal.
